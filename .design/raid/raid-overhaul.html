Salvage Protocol/ Phase 00 — the gate/ Design round, 2026-09-03

Three Ways In

The raid overhaul, settled. Not a rebuild — the tree map, chapters, instancing and run economy all survive untouched. What changes is why you go in: three raid modes, each owning one loot axis and rewarding a different build. And the path choice comes off the screen and into the room — you walk through a door.

Why an overhaul at all

The structure is finished. Four places are hollow.

Raids went through five build-and-playtest rounds and each ended well. Nothing here is broken. What is missing is content in the slots the engine already opened — every finding below was checked against the source, not remembered.

The card pool is four stubs.

One per rarity, all named “Placeholder Buff”, no effects wired — the post-boss pick works and there is nothing to pick. This is the roguelike layer, entirely unbuilt.

RaidConfig.lua:528

The raid shop sells ore bundles.

The same fixed menu every raid, no rotation, selling the exact resource you came in to farm. A shop node is currently a vending machine, not a decision.

NodeConfig.lua:91

Nothing anywhere is tagged RunLocked or Permanent.

The most consequential one. settleRunLoot is fully built and fully inert, so pushing deeper risks nothing and extracting protects nothing — the single decision the whole raid is shaped around has no stakes attached to it yet.

NodeConfig.lua:26

EnemyAI.Patterns has exactly one entry.

Chaser, and all six enemy types point at it. Combat, Ambush and Boss rooms therefore play identically — the only thing that changes between them is how much HP walks at you.

EnemyAI.lua:69

These four are why the modes below are the right shape. Each mode adopts one of the hollow slots as the thing it is about — so filling them stops being four disconnected content chores and becomes three coherent reasons to enter a raid.
The centrepiece

Three modes, one engine

Picked before you spend Energy. Same map generator, same rooms, same combat — different rules, different loot axis, different question you are answering while you play. The point is variety of build: gear that carries a Gauntlet run is the wrong gear for a Salvage Run.

MODE 01

Gauntlet

Cores & Contraband — the power currencies. How far can you push before it kills you?

You are optimising
Card synergy, and when to stop.
Run shape
Endless chapters. One guaranteed Boss per map instead of the current 1–2 rolled, so cards arrive on a rhythm you can plan around.
Cards
The whole point. They stack all run and are always lost on exit — the power is the run, not the account.
Shop sells
Run-only perk accessories, priced in run Scrap. This is where the shop rework lands.
The stake
Payout multiplies with MapsCleared. Die and you keep the base, lose the multiplier.
AI patterns it wants
  • Swarmer — weak, many, flanks rather than beelines.
  • Charger — telegraphed rush, punishing if you stand still.
  • Artillery — ranged, keeps distance, forces you to move.
Build rewarded
Glass cannon. Damage now, scale into the cards, get out before the curve catches you.
MODE 02

Salvage Run

Ore & refined materials — the feedstock the Welding Station and Forge actually eat.

You are optimising
How much to carry against how far the exit is.
Run shape
Shorter maps, no guaranteed Boss, Heal nodes rare. Extraction is a physical room you must reach to bank — not a button.
Carry cap
A real ceiling on ore held. Full bag means choosing what to drop, or leaving now.
Shop sells
Capacity and escape — bag upgrades, a speed boost, one-shot extraction beacons.
The stake
Every ore drop tagged RunLocked. Die or abandon and it is gone. Currency stays exempt, as it already is.
AI patterns it wants
  • Stalker — spawns behind you, slow, patient, does not give up.
  • Guardian — parks on the loot and will not chase. You come to it.
  • Screamer — calls reinforcements if you linger in the room.
Build rewarded
Survivability and movement. Getting out alive is the win condition, not the kill count.
MODE 03

Contract

Blueprints, gun variants, Drone Cores, Contraband — the things you cannot farm anywhere else.

You are optimising
Which contract your current build can actually clear.
Run shape
Fixed length, set by the contract. You know going in how long it is and what it demands.
The board
Rotating offers at difficulty tiers — reach map 3, kill two bosses, clear it without dying — each naming its exact payout.
Shop sells
Objective tools: intel on what is ahead, a re-roll of the remaining map, a revive.
The stake
Reward tagged Permanent — complete the objective and it banks even on a bad exit. Fail it and the trip paid nothing.
AI patterns it wants
  • Defender — holds a point and will not leave it.
  • Runner — flees on sight; the objective is catching it.
  • Sapper — attacks the objective, not you. Ignore it and you fail.
Build rewarded
Specialisation and prep. You pick the loadout for the contract, not a general-purpose one.
Why three modes and not one bigger raid: a single raid has to be balanced for every player at once, so it ends up rewarding one dominant build and quietly retiring the rest. Three narrow modes each get to be unfair in their own direction — and the player choosing between them before spending Energy is itself the strategy layer that raids do not have today.
The second half of the overhaul

The map choice comes off the screen

Today a cleared room opens a GUI and you click a circle. Instead: the room's exit doors unlock, each one labelled with where it goes, and you walk through the one you want. The map GUI stays — it stops being the input and becomes the thing that tells you where you are.

How it works now
  1. Room encounter clears
  2. showMapChoice(state)
  3. RaidMapUpdate → GUI map opens
  4. Player clicks a circle
  5. ChooseRaidNode fires
  6. Teleport to the next room
How it works after
  1. Room encounter clears
  2. unlockExits(state)
  3. Doors light up, one per branch
  4. Player walks through a door
  5. ChooseRaidNode fires — same remote
  6. Teleport to the next room

The door contract

  • ExitDoor New Part name
    A Part in the room Model, one per possible branch. New RaidConfig.ExitDoorName key, following SpawnPointName / InteractPointName exactly.
  • ExitIndex Optional attribute
    A number, deciding which door maps to which branch. Unset on all of them, doors sort by local X instead — so a two-door room needs no attributes at all to behave sensibly.
  • Locked until cleared Behaviour
    Doors are solid and dark while the encounter runs. On resolve each takes its destination type's own NodeTypes.Color and a label with that type's DisplayName — so the information the map circles carried is now on the door itself.
  • Too few doors Fallback
    Warn and open the GUI map for that node. A half-built room must never strand a run — the project's missing-art rule applied to geometry.
  • Too many doors Fallback
    Extras stay sealed and unlit. A room built with three doors works fine in a map that only ever gives it two branches.
  • Leaf nodes Behaviour
    Zero connections, so every door stays sealed and onMapCleared fires exactly as it does today — the chapter transition is unchanged.
  • Fallback room No art yet
    Grows its doors procedurally on the guard rail, the same way it already grows an InteractPoint when none is authored.
The map GUI's new job. A persistent, read-only minimap: where you are, what you cleared, what is ahead. redrawMap's dendrogram layout is kept as-is — it is already proven over 5,000 generated maps to never cross a line, and that guarantee is worth more now that the map is a reference rather than a control.
You asked how to build these

Authoring a raid room in Studio

This is the contract as it exists today — everything except ExitDoor is already live, so you can start building rooms before any of the above is written.

  • ServerStorage.RaidRoomModels Where they live
    One Model per node type, named exactly: Start, Combat, Ambush, Heal, Shop, Boss. Any other name is not found and you get the placeholder square.
  • PrimaryPart Required
    Every room Model must have one, or it silently falls back to the placeholder. The Model is cloned and PivotTo'd to the room origin — so build it centred on the origin, and the pivot is where the player lands.
  • SpawnPoint Combat / Ambush / Boss
    A Part per enemy, each with a String attribute EnemyType set to one of: Scavenger, Raider, Brute, ScrapCrawler, SentinelDrone, VoidwakenHulk. An unrecognised value spawns nothing there and warns — it never guesses. No SpawnPoints at all falls back to the random ring, 50–70 studs out.
  • InteractPoint Heal / Shop
    One Part. A ProximityPrompt is added automatically if it has none — this is what makes the room wait for you instead of firing on entry, and it is where an NPC eventually goes.
  • No Model built Fallback
    A 260×260 concrete square with an invisible guard rail, plus a stand-in InteractPoint on Heal/Shop. Playable, ugly, never blocking.
  • Where rooms sit Instancing
    Built fresh at (0, 800, 0), 600 studs apart per concurrent raid, up to 20 at once. Nothing to line up against — every room is alone in its slot, so rotation never matters.
One gap worth closing while you are in there: every Combat room is the same room. buildRoom looks up one Model per type, so a fifteen-node map walks through the identical box a dozen times. Proposal — RaidRoomModels.Combat may be a Folder of Models instead of a Model, one picked at random per node. Backwards compatible by construction: a Model is used directly, a Folder picks a random Model child. Roughly six lines in buildRoom, and it turns “make the map pretty” from one big build into as many small ones as you feel like making.
Scope guard

What none of this touches

Worth stating plainly, because “overhaul” reads like a rewrite and this is not one. All of the following keeps working exactly as built and verified:

GenerateMap tree + retry Chaptered maps onMapCleared Instancing + slot free-list RunRaidCombat Room-authored spawns settleRunLoot Run currency pool Boss placement rules Ambush run-scaling PlayerActivityService gating Energy cost redrawMap dendrogram
And EnemyAI.lua itself does not change either. Its header already anticipated this: base defense aims TargetPosition at the plot anchor, raids aim it at the player. Every pattern named on this page is one new function in EnemyAI.Patterns plus one config line — which is exactly what that table was built to be.
Needs your call before building

Five open decisions

Each has a recommendation. None of them block starting on step 01 or 02 below.

Do the three modes cost the same Energy?

Recommend Yes, 1 each to start. Different costs are a balance lever you cannot tune before any of them has been played, and an expensive mode nobody picks reads as broken rather than as premium.

Are Gauntlet cards really lost on every exit, including a clean one?

Recommend Yes, always. The moment a card can be banked, Gauntlet becomes a farm for permanent power and every other system has to be balanced around it. Losing them is what makes the run the unit.

Does Salvage Run's carry cap cover currency too?

Recommend No. Currency is already exempt from RunLocked by an explicit earlier decision, and capping it would make the raid shop unusable at exactly the moment you need it.

Where does the Contract board physically live?

Recommend On the existing raid terminal, as the mode-select screen's third tab — not a new tagged Station. It keeps “choose a mode” and “choose a contract” as one decision in one place, and adds no Studio setup.

Do all three modes unlock at once?

Recommend Salvage Run first, the other two behind Research Tiers. It is the mode that feeds crafting, it is the gentlest, and it gives the early-game pacing plan something concrete to hand a new player.

Dependency order — this one really is a sequence

Build order

Doors first because all three modes need them. Salvage Run before Gauntlet because it is almost entirely tagging and reuse. AI patterns last, once there are three different jobs for them to do.

  1. Physical exit doors + read-only minimap
    Mode-agnostic, and the single biggest feel change on the page. ExitDoorName, unlockExits, the GUI map demoted to a reference panel.
    sp-server-dev · sp-config-dev
  2. Room variant folders
    Six lines in buildRoom. Small, independent, and it unblocks every hour of Studio art you want to spend afterwards.
    sp-server-dev
  3. Mode plumbing
    A RaidMode on the raid state, chosen at start, plus one RaidConfig.Modes table holding each mode's rules. Flat table of named strategies — the project's standard shape.
    sp-config-dev → sp-server-dev
  4. Salvage Run
    Cheapest of the three: tag ore RunLocked, add the carry cap, make Extraction a real room. Almost no new systems.
    sp-server-dev · sp-config-dev
  5. Gauntlet
    A real card pool with real effects, plus the run-only perk shop. The biggest content write on the page — and the one that finally spends RollCardChoices.
    sp-config-dev → sp-server-dev
  6. Contract
    Largest new surface: the board, objective tracking, rotation, Permanent payouts. Last because it is the only one that needs a system that does not exist yet.
    sp-server-dev
  7. Enemy AI patterns
    Nine patterns, three per mode. Deliberately last: built against three raids that exist, not one that does not.
    sp-combat-dev

Three directions for the HUD

Every one of these is built from the palette already in HudKit.lua — no invented colours. Each shows the same furniture so they compare fairly: wallet top-left, a rejection toast top-centre, status bottom-left, the action row bottom-centre, and the inventory panel open.

Why this one

Leans hard into the salvage identity. Panels read as steel plates — 2px borders with a lit top edge and a dark bottom, tight 4px radii, an accent bar capping each panel. The primary button is a physical switch with a hard drop shadow under it. The chrome has weight, so buttons look pressable instead of like coloured rectangles.

What it costs

The heaviest of the three. More chrome means less visible screen, and during a wave with the panel open it can start to feel busy. It also commits you hardest to a look — if the game's art direction shifts later, this is the option that fights it.

Why this one

Modern and restrained. Translucent panels over the world, 1px hairlines, big soft radii, wide letterspaced labels and real padding. Accent appears in exactly two places — the primary action and the active tab — so the numbers become the loudest thing on screen. This is the option that stays legible when a lot is happening.

What it costs

The least characterful. It's handsome, but it could belong to any sci-fi game rather than specifically to a grimy salvage one. If you want players to recognise a screenshot as yours, this is the weakest of the three at that job.

Why this one

Everything hugs the screen edges. Angular clipped corners instead of rounded ones, the wallet collapses to a single compact strip, and panels dock to the right edge rather than covering the middle of the fight. Actions become icon buttons with the primary one much larger and glowing. Most distinctive silhouette, and the clearest view of the game during a wave.

What it costs

Icon-led actions have to be learned — a new player sees a shield, not the words "Start Defense", though the labels underneath soften that. The docked panel is also narrower, so the inventory grid drops from 6 columns to 4 and you scroll more.

What happens after you pick

Nothing here is final — these are direction sketches, not the finished HUD. Mixing is fine and probably right: B's spacing with A's button weight, or C's edge-docked panels with B's typography.

Once a direction is settled, the build order is structure first. MainHud.client.lua sits at 164 of Luau's 200 locals, and exceeding that is a compile error that makes the HUD silently never appear — so the panels get extracted into their own ModuleScripts before any restyling begins. Then the tokens and button variants land in HudKit.lua, which restyles every panel at once, and the panels get worked one at a time.

The fonts were chosen to be buildable, not just to look good on this page: Montserrat and Source Sans both exist in Roblox as Enum.Font.Montserrat and Enum.Font.SourceSans, and the mono maps to Enum.Font.Code. Nothing here needs a font you can't actually set in Studio.

The one thing no mockup can show is feedback. Your HUD currently has no hover state, no press state and no tween anywhere. Adding those to the shared button helper will do more for "buttons catch attention" than any of these palettes.

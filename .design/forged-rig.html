Carried over from A

  • Every surface sits in a bevelled frame — a gradient shell from light at the top to near-black at the bottom, so panels read as metal plate rather than flat fills.
  • The accent cap bar across the top of the wallet.
  • Hard offset shadows under buttons and tabs, so they look pressable.
  • The heavy panel header — dark bar, accent tick, 2px divider.

Carried over from C

  • The compact single-strip wallet with icons, instead of three stacked rows.
  • Angular clipped corners everywhere in place of rounded ones.
  • The inventory docked to the right edge rather than covering the middle of the fight.
  • Icon-led actions with the shield as the oversized primary, labels underneath.
  • The segmented integrity bar and the corner bracket framing the top-left.

Worth deciding before this gets built

The shield got bigger and harder. It's 120×78 now, with a bevelled frame, a 5px drop shadow and the glow kept from C. It is unambiguously the loudest thing on screen — which is right for the main action, but tell me if it tips into too much.

The bevel is a real cost in Roblox. A gradient frame with an inset surface means two nested frames per panel instead of one, plus a UIGradient on each. That's fine at this element count, but it roughly doubles the instances behind every panel — worth knowing before it's the house style.

Angular corners are cheap, but not free. Roblox has no clip-path. Clipped corners come from either a 9-slice ImageLabel (one image asset, scales cleanly, my recommendation) or small rotated corner pieces. Rounded corners via UICorner would be simpler — so this is the one choice here that costs real setup, and it's the most distinctive thing in the design.
